Why This Matters to You

If your face or voice has ever appeared anywhere online, social media, a news article, a podcast, a YouTube video, then your likeness is already out there. And right now, you have almost no way to know who's using it, where, or how.

Whair gives you three things no one else offers together:

  1. Proof that it's yours, a timestamped, cryptographic record that proves you owned your photos and voice recordings before anyone else used them.
  2. The ability to find unauthorized use, Whair scans specific pages or the entire internet and shows you everywhere your face appears.
  3. A way to do something about it, generate a ready-to-submit takedown package for any platform (Instagram, YouTube, TikTok, X), or choose to license it and get paid instead.

Let's walk through each of these so you can see how they work in practice.

1. Register Your Identity

The first thing Whair needs is to know what you look like. You upload a few clear photos of your face, and Whair creates a secure reference set, like a digital fingerprint of your likeness that it uses to find you across the internet.

Real-Life Example

A recording artist uploads 6 photos of herself, different outfits, different lighting, some from photoshoots, some casual. Now Whair knows her face. From this point on, if her image shows up on any website Whair scans, it will recognize her and flag it.

Try it now:

1

In the sidebar, click Identity → Enroll.

2

Enter your display name (your real name or stage name).

3

Drag and drop 5 or more clear photos of your face into the upload area. Use different angles and lighting for best results, front-facing, side profile, natural light, studio light.

4

Click Enroll. Wait a few seconds. You'll see a success message when it's done.

What just happened behind the scenes: Each photo was hashed (a unique digital fingerprint was created), cryptographically signed (proving you uploaded it at this exact time), and registered in Whair's system as YOUR likeness. If anyone ever disputes ownership, you have proof.

You can view all your registered photos anytime under Identity → Assets. You can add more photos later or remove ones you don't want.

2. Register Your Voice

Voice cloning is one of the fastest-growing threats in AI. Someone can take a short clip of you speaking and create a synthetic version of your voice that says anything they want. The first step to protecting against this is establishing that the voice is yours.

Real-Life Example

An influencer records a 20-second clip of herself speaking naturally and uploads it. Now there's a timestamped, signed record proving this is her real voice, not an AI clone. If a deepfake version of her voice surfaces later, she has provenance proof that her original voice was registered first.

Try it now:

1

In the sidebar, click Identity → Voice.

2

Click Add Voice Sample. Give it a name (e.g. "Interview clip" or "Voice memo") and upload an audio file (MP3, WAV, etc.), even 10–30 seconds is enough.

3

Click Upload Sample. You'll see it appear in your voice library with a green "Ready" badge.

Note: Voice registration currently stores and signs your voice samples as proof of ownership. Active voice scanning, where Whair automatically finds AI clones of your voice across the internet, is coming in a future update (more on that at the end of this guide).

3. Prove You Own It

Ownership disputes happen all the time online. Someone reposts your photo and claims it's theirs. A brand uses your image and says they found it on a stock site. Without proof of original ownership, these fights are hard to win.

Whair's verification system creates a tamper-proof record, like getting a document notarized, but digital and instant.

Real-Life Example

A model signs her portfolio photos in Whair on March 12. Two months later, she finds a clothing brand using one of those photos on their website without permission. She can prove she owned the photo before the brand used it, her Whair record shows the exact date, time, and cryptographic hash. That's admissible evidence.

Try it now:

1

Go to Verification → Sign. Select one of your enrolled photos and click Sign Asset. You'll see the cryptographic signature and timestamp.

2

Go to Verification → Verify. Upload the same photo you just signed. Whair will confirm: "Verified, this file was registered by [Your Name] on [Date]."

3

Now try uploading a random image from the internet. Whair will say "No match found", because it was never registered.

Your full signing history is always available under Verification → History.

4. Find Where Your Face Appears Online

This is where Whair gets powerful. Once you've registered your identity, you can search the internet for your face. Whair offers two ways to do this:

Option A: Scan a specific page

If you suspect a particular website or article is using your image, you can scan it directly.

Real-Life Example

An actress sees a blog article about her and suspects they're using her photos without a license. She pastes the URL into Whair, hits Scan, and within seconds sees that 3 of the 8 images on the page match her face with 94% confidence. Each match is automatically logged as an incident she can act on.

Try it now:

1

Go to Detection → Scan URL.

2

Paste any URL that contains photos of a face you enrolled. For testing, try a celebrity fan page or news article (if you enrolled with a public figure's photos for testing purposes).

3

Click Start Scan. Wait 15–60 seconds. You'll see how many images were found on the page, how many faces were detected, and how many matched your registered identity.

Option B: Search the entire internet

This is the big one. Instead of checking one page at a time, you can search the entire web for everywhere your image appears.

Real-Life Example

A fitness influencer wants to know everywhere her photos are being used. She selects her best headshot, clicks "Search," and Google's AI finds her image on 47 different websites, including 3 she never authorized. Stock photo sites, overseas e-commerce stores, random blogs. She had no idea.

Try it now:

1

Go to Detection → Web Search.

2

You'll see your enrolled reference photos. Click on the clearest one.

3

Click Search for this image. Wait about 30–60 seconds while Google scans the web.

The results show you: pages that contain your image, exact matches (pixel-perfect copies), partial matches (cropped or edited versions), and any labels or entities Google identified. Each discovered page automatically creates an incident in your dashboard.

Reviewing what was found

After any scan or search, go to Detection → Incidents to see every match. Each incident shows the source URL, the platform, match confidence (e.g. 94%), and status. Click into any incident for full details.

5. Take It Down

Finding unauthorized use is only half the battle. The other half is getting it removed. Normally, filing a DMCA takedown means navigating confusing platform forms, gathering evidence, writing legal statements, it can take hours per violation. Whair does it in one click.

Real-Life Example

A content creator finds her face being used on a scam Instagram ad. From the incident page in Whair, she clicks "Create Takedown." Whair automatically assembles an evidence packet: her identity, the source URL, match confidence, timestamps, provenance proof, and a statement of rights. She copies it into Instagram's report form, submits, and the post is removed within 48 hours. Total time: 5 minutes.

Try it now:

1

Go to Detection → Incidents and click on any incident.

2

Click Create Takedown. Whair generates a full evidence packet with everything the platform needs.

3

You'll see a link to the correct platform's report form (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok, or X). In a real scenario, you'd click through, paste the evidence, and submit.

Track the status of all your takedowns under Enforcement → Cases. Each case moves through stages: Draft → Submitted → Acknowledged → Resolved.

6. Get Paid Instead

Not every unauthorized use needs to be taken down. Sometimes it's smarter to get paid. If a brand or website is using your image and it's actually helping your visibility, you can offer them a license instead of a takedown threat.

Real-Life Example

A musician discovers a blog using her concert photo as their header image. Instead of a takedown, she creates a license through Whair: $200 for non-exclusive use for one year. The blog owner pays through Stripe, and the musician earns money from content that was already out there. Everyone wins.

Try it now:

1

Go to Monetization → Licenses. This is where you can create, view, and manage licenses.

2

Check out Monetization → Connect to see the Stripe integration, this is where you'd connect your bank account to receive payments.

3

Visit Monetization → Earnings to see the earnings dashboard where revenue from licenses would appear.

Note: Monetization is available in the app for you to explore. The full payment flow (where a licensee actually pays and you receive funds) is still being finalized. For now, you can see how it will work and create test licenses.

7. Other Features Worth Knowing About

These are smaller features that round out the platform. You don't need to test each one step-by-step, just know they're there.

  • Automated Monitoring (Monitoring in the sidebar), Set up recurring scans on specific URLs. For example, schedule a daily scan of TMZ's page about you, and Whair will automatically flag new unauthorized uses without you lifting a finger.
  • Whitelist / Authorized Sources (Settings → Whitelist), Add domains or accounts you've authorized to use your likeness (your own website, your management company, brand partners). Whair won't flag these as violations.
  • Profile & Security Settings (Settings), Update your display name, change your password, and manage your account security.
  • Scan Results History (Detection → Results), A log of every scan you've ever run, with status, image counts, and match counts.
