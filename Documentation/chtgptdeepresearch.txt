#### entity["company","Amazon Web Services","cloud provider"] Rekognition capabilities, pricing, and gotchas

**Core face APIs and constraints**

The Rekognition `DetectFaces` API detects faces and returns bounding boxes and attributes; it is most effective on frontal faces and can return up to 100 faces per image. citeturn23search6  
The `CompareFaces` API compares a source face against faces in a target image and returns similarity matches. citeturn23search2turn23search10

AWS documents minimum face-size guidance (faces as small as 50×50 pixels in up to 1920×1080 images) and notes accuracy improves with larger faces and good lighting. citeturn23search36

**Pricing (as shown in AWS materials)**

AWS has described its Image API pricing as tiered per image, with a free tier and per-image rates by volume for grouped APIs (including CompareFaces and DetectFaces) in its pricing update materials. citeturn24search2turn24search27

For video, AWS pricing examples show:

- Rekognition **Streaming Video Events** label detection at **$0.00817 per minute processed** (example pricing shown). citeturn25view0turn25view2  
- Rekognition **Stored Video Analysis** label detection at **$0.10 per minute**, shot detection at **$0.05 per minute**, and content moderation at **$0.10 per minute** (example pricing shown). citeturn25view0  

**Quotas and beta-scale feasibility**

AWS publishes default TPS quotas by region; for example, DetectFaces/CompareFaces can be 100 TPS in some regions (default), with quota increase requests possible. citeturn25view3

**Beta gotchas**

- Rekognition uses EXIF orientation for JPEGs (can affect bounding boxes) and does not apply the same correction to PNGs or JPGs without orientation metadata, which can cause mismatch issues in downstream cropping pipelines. citeturn23search2turn23search6

### Verification pillar

#### C2PA and Content Credentials implementation detail

The C2PA initiative provides an “open technical standard” for establishing provenance and edit history (“Content Credentials”). citeturn26search15turn26search4  
The Content Authenticity Initiative describes Content Credentials as “verifiable metadata” based on C2PA standards, containing information like who produced content, when, and what tools/edits were applied (a “nutrition label” framing). citeturn26search2turn26search19turn26search25

**Spec and SDK**

- The C2PA “Content Credentials” specification is published (PDF versions exist; the run retrieved a 2.1 PDF and the C2PA site also lists newer spec indexes). citeturn26search0turn26search4  
- Open-source SDKs exist: the Rust library `c2pa-rs` can “create and sign C2PA claims and manifests.” citeturn26search1  
- Mobile support exists (example: “C2PA Android” docs describe reading, validation, and creation, with multiple signing methods and Android Keystore/StrongBox support). citeturn26search11  

**What “sign” and “verify” mean operationally**

At a high level (and consistent with CAI descriptions), signing attaches cryptographically verifiable provenance information; verification checks integrity/tampering and displays metadata. citeturn26search25turn26search2  
C2PA implementation guidance notes creating a manifest is not “lightweight” because it involves digital signatures and potentially retrieving credentials; it recommends doing it only at significant lifecycle events (create/export). citeturn26search27

**Adoption signals you can weaponize in positioning**

Mainstream platform adoption is still uneven, but there are tangible signals:

- Reporting indicates YouTube began implementing a “captured with a camera” label based on C2PA, and only devices/tools supporting C2PA 2.1+ can generate the needed metadata for that label. citeturn26news39turn26search0  
- Industry reporting describes broader ecosystem support (Adobe/Microsoft/Google) but limited consistent display on social platforms. citeturn26news41turn26news42  

**Actionable Whair beta implication**

A verification-forward beta can ship fast by offering:

- A “Verified Asset” flow: creator uploads canonical headshots/voiceprints + optional authorized media; you produce signed “authorized” outputs and a verifier view (even if platforms don’t display it yet). citeturn26search1turn26search27turn26search2  
- A “Provenance viewer” inside Whair: parse and display C2PA manifests using the SDK; treat it as a differentiator when your Detection finds matches that lack provenance. citeturn26search1turn26search2turn26search11

## Regulatory and legal landscape relevant to Whair

### Tennessee ELVIS Act

The ELVIS Act is commonly described as a Tennessee law designed to protect artists—especially musicians—from unauthorized AI voice replication and related “audio deepfakes,” and mainstream reporting describes it as the first U.S. state law of its kind, signed by Governor Bill Lee and effective July 1, 2024. citeturn29news41turn29news42turn29search10

Industry coverage also frames it as expanding Tennessee’s personal rights regime to explicitly include “voice” and broaden protections beyond narrow advertising contexts (summaries vary by source). citeturn29search14turn29search26turn29search22
