Deciphered acronym to deliver straightforward explanation

C2PA stands for Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity. It's an open standard backed by Adobe, Microsoft, Google, and 300+ other organizations that lets you cryptographically sign content to prove where it came from, who made it, and whether it's been tampered with. Think of it like a nutrition label for digital content.

In Whair's context this is huge because it's the tech that would power your Verification pillar. When a creator registers their likeness through Whair, you could sign that content with C2PA metadata so there's a provable, tamper-proof record that says "this person owns this face/voice and authorized this specific use." Then if someone deepfakes them or uses their likeness without consent, you can point to the verified original vs the unauthorized copy and there's a cryptographic paper trail.

The reason it's your white space is that none of your competitors are using it. Loti and Helos are focused on finding misuse and filing takedowns but they don't give creators a way to prove ownership with an industry standard. And the fact that YouTube is already starting to use C2PA based labels means the ecosystem is moving toward this, so you'd be building on something that platforms are actually beginning to adopt rather than something proprietary that nobody recognizes.
